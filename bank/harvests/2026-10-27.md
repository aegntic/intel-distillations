## New signals (vs the wiki as of 2026-09-01)

1. **Software-factory pattern.** Cole Medin predicts it becomes the standard way software is built within a year or two: define intent up front (the agent grills you), split a release into issues, agents run the plan-implement-validate loop, human reviews output in batch in staging [f02 11:57, 10:24]. Building it yourself is the highest-leverage builder move; out-of-the-box versions come later for everyone else [f02 12:23].
2. **Batch check-ins.** Check background agents about once a day per stream instead of monitoring continuously, and spend the recovered time making the harness more autonomous: rules, skills, codified validation so it can run a day or more [f02 13:10, 9:20, 10:13].
3. **Attention is the binding constraint.** Human cognitive bandwidth does not parallelize; one creator reports ~100x more context switches than 5 years ago [unverified]; guilt while waiting on an agent is what spawns yet another session [f02 1:42, 0:40, 3:05].
4. **One primary session.** Near-instant model responses (hundreds of tokens per second) are predicted to collapse working style into a single orchestrating session instead of many parallel conversations [f02 14:05, 13:56].
5. **Card-level spend rails.** Enforce agent budgets at the payment instrument, not in the prompt: Revolut Business virtual cards with the limits on the card itself ($50 weekly, software purchases only, region-locked, end-of-month expiry) plus a webhook fired on every charge [f02 3:47, 3:31].
6. **Playbook repos outlive chat.** Recurring workflows ship as forkable GitHub repos containing readme, system prompt, examples, and evals; chat history decays through truncation and compaction, repos do not [f05 3:08, 42:45].
7. **Slop is the differentiator problem.** Unmodified copy-paste output reads as slop; customizing and merging workflows is the differentiator [f05 53:35]. Own face and voice make a channel defensible, and channel momentum is the real asset an AI-slop flag resets [f05 57:03, 58:33]. Independently, Riley Brown labels avatar content "the slop category" [f06 56:04].
8. **Five 2027 skills.** Taste, agency, focus, resourcefulness, follow-through each gained leverage as AI improved [f03 0:49]; done means "someone benefits", not "prototype works", and a rough first version surfaces requirements planning cannot [f03 8:24, 7:42].
9. **Feature-count law.** Each additional feature makes a vibe-coded app about 10x harder to maintain and more confusing to users [f06 1:36:16].
10. **Attention economics, macro version.** Galloway's intervention opens with a screen-time audit (10-50 recoverable hours per week in Twitter, TikTok, porn, trading apps) and names frictionless digital substitutes, not sentience, as the gravest near-term AI risk [f07 35:54, 16:53].

## Concept updates

### update: context-discipline

The corpus adds a human-side law to token-side discipline: cognitive bandwidth does not parallelize, and parallel agent sessions are what force the switching [f02 1:42]. The named failure mode is guilt while waiting, which opens extra sessions and compounds the problem [f02 3:05]. The operating fix is batch check-ins, about once a day per stream, with recovered attention reinvested into harness autonomy [f02 13:10, 9:20]. Cole Medin predicts near-instant responses will collapse working style into one primary orchestrating session [f02 14:05]. His /goal verdict: a goal statement alone cannot encode how you personally babysit and validate work, so validation lives in the harness [f02 10:52].

### update: sub-agents-and-parallelism

New tension, not a replacement. The 09-01 file prescribes fanning work out one sub-agent per deliverable [v09 10:15]; Cole Medin says the human should run one primary session and stop juggling [f02 13:56]. Reconciliation candidate: parallelism belongs below the human, in background workers reviewed in batch, not in multiple foreground conversations competing for one attention budget. His software factory operationalizes it: a manager agent splits a release into issues, workers run plan-implement-validate autonomously, the human reviews the output once in staging [f02 11:57]. He concedes some people will end up juggling multiple factories anyway [f02 15:23].

### update: definition-of-done

Independent convergence on the finish-line thesis. Nate B Jones defines done as someone benefiting, not a working prototype, and says engineers who stop at "it shipped" are wrong: own whether the software is useful [f03 8:24, 20:02]. His rough-first-version method inverts planning: an agent's imperfect draft (deals with 14 days of no activity) surfaces requirements the buyer could not state up front [f03 7:42]. Cole Medin explains why the finish line must live outside the model: /goal alone cannot encode how you validate, so rules, skills, and evals are what let an agent run for a day or more [f02 10:52, 10:13].

### update: agent-business-models

Two operational additions. First, spend rails: enforce agent budgets at the card, not in the prompt; virtual cards carry the limits themselves plus a webhook on every charge [f02 3:47, 3:31]. That is a standardizable artifact for every client install. Second, a delivery-model claim worth selling against: the software-factory pattern (grilled intent, autonomous issue loop, batch review) is predicted to become the standard way software is built within a year or two [f02 11:57], which makes the studio's own harness the product demo. Vendor-economics caution travels with this corpus: VideoExpress one-time pricing and its 600% GPU-cost claim are unverified vendor assertions [f05 39:07, 38:55] [unverified].

### update: voice-and-brand-laws

The slop question is now triangulated by three sources. Ponna: unmodified copy-paste output is the slop failure mode; customizing workflows differentiates [f05 53:35]. His positive law: your own face and voice make a channel, in his words, impossible to demonetize as AI slop, and channel momentum is the asset a slop flag resets [f05 57:03, 58:33]. Riley Brown independently calls AI-avatar content "the slop category", says HeyGen output still looks dead, and credits B-roll-heavy formats as what works [f06 56:04]. This strengthens the AI-drafts-90/human-finishes-10 resolution and weakens the niche-catalog claim that AI avatars are monetization-safe [NAB9PyN2-3E 10:36].

### update: validation-tactics

The rough first version is a validation tactic, not just a build tactic: build the ugly working artifact to extract requirements a stakeholder could not articulate, then let them react to it [f03 7:42, 8:24]. It pairs with a scope control: with identical tools, one person starts 15 projects in a month and quits each when work gets awkward, the other stays on one customer problem; sustained focus is underweighted in AI conversations [f03 11:44, 9:39]. Anti-goal for the council checklist: AI can make distraction look productive, many prototypes and three plans by 5pm, none far enough to matter [f03 12:31].

## Proposed new concepts

**focus-and-attention-economics: create it.** Four of ten sources converge on human attention as the binding constraint on agent leverage, and context-discipline covers token economics, not attention economics. Proposed scope: attention as the scarce input (the Newport scarcity quote [f02 1:31]); the parallel-session diagnosis and batch cadence [f02 1:42, 13:10]; screen-time audit and reallocation method [f07 35:54]; news-cutting as attention defense, keep one or two causes you can influence [f04 36:13]; deep-focus pressure valves like deliberate games [f02 8:03]; and the pressure law, capability gains raise output expectations in step [f02 2:59].

## Contradictions with the 09-01 wiki

- **Session topology.** 09-01 prescribes fan-out, one sub-agent per deliverable, 10-15 isolated sessions on a control graph [v09 10:15; v18 3:36]. This corpus says one primary orchestrating session, stop juggling [f02 13:56]. Riley Brown predicts the opposite working style again: CEOs multi-tabling four Manus agents across screens within a year [f06 1:18:08]. Candidate reconciliation (one human session, many background workers, batch review) is untested, not settled.
- **Autonomy timeline.** 09-01 holds the human remains the finish line for business work, with approval gates on anything client-facing [VdG2o91D_2k 16:28]. Cole Medin agrees for open-ended work: LLMs and harnesses are mostly not capable enough to run unattended [f02 5:21]. But Ponna and Riley Brown ship end-to-end agent-run media workflows today [f05 3:08; f06 1:12:48]. Probably task-type-dependent (open-ended coding vs template-bound media), so scope the 09-01 rule instead of discarding it.
- **Avatar safety.** 09-01 carries "AI avatars are monetization-safe except legal/finance/health advice" [NAB9PyN2-3E 10:36]. This corpus: avatar content is "the slop category" and HeyGen output still looks dead [f06 56:04], with face-and-voice named as the demonetization defense [f05 57:03, 58:33]. The newer, more cautious position should win until counter-evidence arrives.
